Adjuration

ACTOR_TERM / RITUAL

Definition

Actor Term (from Latin adjurare, to swear by, to compel by oath). The adjuration is the ritual speech act by which a practitioner compels a spirit — demon, angel, or ambiguous intermediate being — to appear, obey, or depart, typically by invoking divine names, Solomonic authority, or binding oaths sworn in God's name. It differs from prayer (oratio), which petitions; from invocation, which calls; and from suffumigation or physical rite, which acts materially. The adjuration is the central operative verbal mode of the Solomonic grimoire tradition.

Structure and Examples

A typical adjuration follows a recognizable pattern: address to the spirit by name and title; statement of the divine authority by which the practitioner speaks ("I adjure you by the living God, by the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, by the seal of Solomon..."); specification of what the spirit is commanded to do; and often a threat of what angel or divine name will punish the spirit's non-compliance. The spirit catalogues of the Testament of Solomon, the Clavicula Salomonis, and the Lemegeton organize their material around adjuration formulas keyed to specific spirits.

Jewish adjuration (Hebrew hashba'ah) appears in the Sepher ha-Razim, the magical papyri, and Geonic-period amulet texts analysed by Gideon Bohak (Ancient Jewish Magic, 2008). The Arabic tradition of Solomonic adjuration is preserved in texts studied by Pablo Torijano (Solomon the Esoteric King, 2002). The Latin tradition, from the Clavicula Salomonis through the Munich Manual's conjuration rituals (Kieckhefer, Forbidden Rites, 1997), represents the medieval crystallization of this tradition.

Theological Status

The adjuration of spirits was theologically contested: the adjuration of demons was universally condemned as an implicit demonic pact; the adjuration of angels was more ambiguous. The Ars Notoria addresses prayers to God and angels in ways that blend petition and adjuration; its condemnation by Thomas Aquinas turned partly on the question of whether the formulas addressed divine power or presupposed demonic assistance. Richard Kieckhefer's analysis of the Munich Manual distinguishes levels of coerciveness in the conjuration formulas as evidence for the spectrum of relations with spirits that practitioners imagined.
