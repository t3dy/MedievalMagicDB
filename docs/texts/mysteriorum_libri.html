Mysteriorum Libri: John Dee's Angelic Conversations

John Dee's Mysteriorum Libri (Books of Mysteries), together with related diaries and records, documents one of the most remarkable projects of spirit communication in the history of Western esotericism. Beginning in 1583 and continuing intermittently until the early 1590s, Dee and his scryer Edward Kelley conducted hundreds of sessions in which Kelley gazed into a crystal or obsidian mirror (the "shewstone") and reported communications from angels who identified themselves variously as Uriel, Michael, Gabriel, Nalvage, and others. These communications were recorded in elaborate detail by Dee himself, producing a voluminous archive of angelic dictations that included a new language (Enochian), a system of tables and calls for communicating with celestial beings, and prophecies about European politics and the imminent end of the world.

The Dee-Kelley sessions operated within a specific theological framework: Dee understood himself not as a sorcerer but as a pious Christian philosopher seeking authentic divine communication in the tradition of the biblical prophets and the ancient patriarchs who had received divine wisdom directly from God. His angels were presented not as the demons of the grimoire tradition but as genuine celestial beings conveying authentic heavenly knowledge to a chosen recipient. This self-presentation allowed Dee to pursue what was functionally spirit communication while maintaining his sense of himself as a legitimate religious and philosophical investigator.

Deborah Harkness's groundbreaking study John Dee's Conversations with Angels (1999) provided the first comprehensive scholarly analysis of the angelic diaries, situating them within the intellectual, religious, and political context of Elizabethan England and continental European esotericism. Her analysis demonstrated that the diaries are neither records of deliberate fraud (as some have argued) nor evidence of Dee's credulity, but products of a sophisticated intellectual project that engaged seriously with contemporary questions about the nature of knowledge, divine communication, and the reformation of learning. The Dee-Kelley sessions represent a culminating expression of the Renaissance magical tradition's aspiration to recover pristine ancient wisdom through direct spiritual contact.
