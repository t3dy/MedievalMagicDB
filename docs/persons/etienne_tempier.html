Étienne Tempier

THEOLOGIAN / MEDIEVAL

Life

Étienne Tempier (d. 1279) was appointed Bishop of Paris in 1268, after a career as a master of theology at Paris. His tenure as bishop coincided with the height of the "radical Aristotelianism" controversy: the teaching by Siger of Brabant and others of philosophical positions derived from Aristotle and Averroes that seemed to conflict with Christian doctrine, including the eternity of the world, the unity of the intellect (there is only one intellect for all humanity, not individual intellects), and the necessary determination of events by celestial causes. Tempier responded to this controversy with two rounds of condemnations that permanently shaped the intellectual context of the medieval and Renaissance university.

The Condemnations of 1270 and 1277

The condemnation of 1270 (thirteen theses) targeted the most immediately problematic propositions associated with radical Aristotelianism, including the unity of the intellect, the eternity of the world, and determinism about celestial causation. The condemnation of 1277 (219 theses) was far more sweeping. Issued on 7 March 1277 — the third anniversary of Thomas Aquinas's death, a coincidence that generated immediate comment — the 219 condemned theses included propositions concerning free will and celestial determination, the limits of divine omnipotence, the impossibility of multiple worlds, and many others. Among those condemned were propositions associated with Aquinas himself, though Aquinas was not named.

For the history of magic, the condemnations are significant for their treatment of celestial causation: several condemned theses assert that celestial bodies necessarily determine terrestrial events including human choices (propositions 161, 162, 207 in the standard enumeration). Condemning astrological determinism forced subsequent natural philosophers to be more careful about how they articulated the relationship between stellar influence and human freedom — which in turn constrained how they could theorize the causal basis of astrological and talismanic magic. Nicolas Weill-Parot's work on addressative magic analyses these effects in detail.
